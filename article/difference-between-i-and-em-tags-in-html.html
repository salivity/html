Difference Between i and em Tags in HTML

While both the <i> and <em> tags render text in italics by default in web browsers, they serve distinct semantic purposes, specifically regarding verbal emphasis. This article explains how the <em> tag applies linguistic stress that changes the spoken meaning of a sentence and alters screen reader inflection, whereas the <i> tag represents an alternate voice or tone without adding spoken emphasis.

The <em> Tag: Semantic Verbal Emphasis

The <em> tag stands for "emphasis" and is specifically designed to indicate stress emphasis in spoken text. Applying <em> to a word changes the inflection and linguistic focus of the entire sentence.

Consider how shifting verbal emphasis alters meaning in spoken dialogue:

  • I did not say you stole my money. (Someone else said it.)
  • I did not say you stole my money. (I implied it, but didn't say it.)
  • I did not say you stole my money. (I said someone else stole it.)

In HTML, this stress is marked up using <em>:

<p>I did not say <em>you</em> stole my money.</p>

Because <em> denotes verbal stress, assistive technologies such as screen readers are designed to recognize it. Modern screen readers can alter their audio output—raising pitch, changing cadence, or increasing volume—to audibly reflect the stressed word to visually impaired users.

The <i> Tag: Alternate Voice or Tone

The <i> tag historically represented "italics" purely for visual styling, but in modern HTML5, it represents "idiomatic text." It marks text that is offset from the normal prose to indicate an alternate voice, mood, or context, but without adding stress or verbal emphasis.

Common use cases for the <i> tag include:

  • Technical terms or definitions
  • Idiomatic phrases or words from another language (e.g., quid pro quo)
  • Internal dialogue or thoughts
  • Taxonomic designations or species names (e.g., Homo sapiens)
  • Names of vessels, ships, or creative works
<p>The Latin term <i>in situ</i> refers to something being examined in its original place.</p>

In this example, the phrase in situ requires visual differentiation to signify foreign phrasing, but it does not demand verbal stress. A screen reader reading this sentence reads it in a flat, natural cadence without emphasizing the words.

Key Differences Summary

  • Linguistic Stress: <em> alters the verbal stress and meaning of a sentence; <i> does not.
  • Accessibility: Screen readers change audio inflection and pitch for <em>, but generally read <i> with standard cadence.
  • Semantic Definition: <em> represents structural, linguistic emphasis; <i> represents an alternate voice, technical notation, or stylistic convention.
  • Visual Rendering: Both tags default to an italic font style in all modern browsers.

Practical Rule of Thumb

If speaking the sentence out loud requires you to raise your voice, stress the syllables, or change your pitch on a specific word to convey the proper meaning, use <em>. If you are italicizing text solely to mark a title, foreign phrase, thought, or taxonomy term without changing the spoken stress of the sentence, use <i>.
